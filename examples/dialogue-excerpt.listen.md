## The problem with noise {#the-problem-with-noise}

@host
Ordinary headphones fight noise by brute force — foam and plastic that physically block sound waves from reaching your ear. That works well for a hiss, badly for the low rumble of a jet engine.

@guest
Why does the rumble get through so easily?

@host
Because low frequencies have long wavelengths — sometimes longer than the headphone cushion itself. No amount of passive padding stops a wave that big.

@guest
So how do the "smart" ones get around that?

## Fighting sound with sound

@host
They don't block the wave — they cancel it. A tiny microphone on the outside of the earcup listens to the incoming noise, a chip flips that signal upside down in real time, and a speaker plays the inverted wave alongside your music.

@guest
And the two waves just erase each other?

@host
Almost exactly. Where the original wave pushes air outward, the inverted one pulls it back in, at the same instant. The two cancel, and what reaches your eardrum is close to silence — engine rumble gone, your music untouched.

@guest
That's a strange kind of magic — fighting sound with more sound.

@host
It's also why it works best on steady, predictable noise like an engine drone, and struggles with a sudden clatter — there's no time to compute the opposite wave before the sound has already passed.
